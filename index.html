Information Science

Why Information Science Is the Most Underrated Degree of the Digital Age

5 min read · January 2025

When people ask me what I study and I say "Information Science," I usually get one of two reactions: a polite nod — translation, they have no idea what that means — or a quick "Oh, so like… computers?"

Not exactly. And that's precisely the problem. Information Science sits at this beautiful, complicated intersection of human knowledge, technology, and society — and somehow, it's managed to stay almost invisible in a world drowning in data.

Here's what most people don't realize: every Google search you run, every library catalogue you browse, every time your streaming platform recommends the exact film you wanted — that's information science at work. The structures that make data findable, retrievable, and useful don't build themselves.

We live in an information age. The people who understand how information flows, how it's organized, and how to make it accessible are perhaps the most valuable people in the room. We just don't always call them that.

So next time someone asks what Information Science is, maybe the answer is simply this: we make the world make sense. And in 2025, that's not a small thing at all.

Student Life

Surviving Your First Year at OOU (The Honest Guide)

4 min read · October 2024

Let me be upfront: your first year at Olabisi Onabanjo University will not look like a Nollywood movie about campus life. It'll be messier, more confusing, and — if you let it — more beautiful than any movie could capture.

The first thing nobody tells you is that orientation week is both overwhelming and completely useless for actually orienting yourself. Pay attention to the seniors instead. Not all of them — just the calm ones who look like they sleep regularly.

Find your classes before the first week starts. Walk the routes. Know where the ICT centre is, where the faculty building is, and — critically — where the nearest decent food is. These three things will save your sanity more than any advice your departmental adviser gives you.

On coursework: read ahead. Not by chapters and chapters — just enough to know what's coming. It makes lectures land differently when the vocabulary isn't totally foreign.

The most important thing? Don't try to survive alone. Find your people. Not necessarily your roommates — though that helps when it works. Find the people who care about the same things you do. They exist. OOU is big enough to hide them but small enough that you'll find them if you look.

What AI Means for the Future of Libraries

6 min read · March 2025

There's a running narrative that AI is going to kill libraries. That when every book is digitized and every question can be answered by a chatbot in seconds, the library — and by extension, the librarian — becomes redundant.

I want to push back on that. Not defensively, but with genuine curiosity about what happens when you combine two of the most interesting forces of the 21st century: artificial intelligence and the organized memory of humanity.

AI is extraordinary at retrieval. It can surface information at a speed and scale no human librarian can match. But it is not extraordinary — yet — at context, ethics, or understanding why a particular piece of information matters to a particular person in a particular moment. That is very much still human work.

What the best libraries of the future will look like, I think, is this: AI handling the first layer — the fast, the broad, the obvious. And librarians handling the deeper layer — curation, verification, community connection, and the profoundly human act of helping someone figure out what they actually need to know.

Far from killing libraries, AI might be the thing that finally frees librarians to do their most important work: thinking.
